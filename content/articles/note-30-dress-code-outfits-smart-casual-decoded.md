Dress code outfits get over 160,000 searches a month, and the confusion is understandable: smart casual is the most common dress code and the least explained.

Here is the 60-second decode. Smart casual means one polished piece plus one relaxed piece, never two of either. Tailored trousers with a simple knit. A blazer over good jeans. A midi skirt with clean sneakers. The moment both pieces are polished, you have drifted into business; the moment both are relaxed, you are just casual.

The test is the swap test. Take the outfit you would wear at home, change exactly one piece to something structured — the trousers, the jacket, the shoe — and stop there. One upgrade is smart casual. Two upgrades is a different dress code.

Cocktail attire works the same way, one step up: one dressy piece, everything else quiet. A satin top with straight trousers reads cocktail; satin top plus sequins plus heels reads party, which is a different invitation entirely.

The practice: before your next "smart casual" event, name your one polished piece out loud. If you cannot name it, add it. If you named two, take one off. Sixty seconds, no second-guessing, and you walk in exactly dressed enough.
