Baggy suit is up +90% this winter, and the appeal is easy to see: oversized tailoring that still looks intentional gives you the comfort of a relaxed fit with the polish of a suit. The wide-leg trouser and the soft, dropped-shoulder jacket move like loungewear but read like an outfit — no stiff shoulders, no tugging waistband, just a clean, generous line from top to toe. It is the suit for days when you want to look put-together without feeling put together.

The fit rule is what keeps it intentional instead of sloppy. Let the suit be the only oversized thing in the outfit: one baggy suit, one quiet base, and the look reads deliberate. Keep everything underneath slim and simple — a fitted top, a plain tee, a narrow belt if the jacket needs a waist — and let the trousers break cleanly over a pointed shoe or a low heel. Stack another oversized layer on top and the shape disappears; keep the base neat and the volume reads like a choice, not an accident.

It earns its keep across the wardrobe. In chocolate brown or camel it reads like quiet luxury — the new neutral doing the work of a whole outfit — while black keeps it sharp enough for an evening out. An oversized blazer worn open over the same trousers turns it into a second look, and a lace bandana knotted at the throat keeps the tailoring from feeling too severe. One baggy suit, one quiet base, and winter's loosest tailoring does the work for you: relaxed, polished, and never sloppy.
