Silk scarves as tops are trending in New York and São Paulo, and the appeal is obvious: one scarf, three tops, and a whole outfit that costs nothing new. The trick is not the scarf itself, it is the fold — get that right and the look reads intentional, get it wrong and it reads like a beach cover-up in the wrong season.

Here is the one rule that keeps it elegant. Fold the scarf into a clean triangle and let the point sit flat, never bunched at the neck or twisted at the waist. A flat triangle reads like a designed top; a bunched one reads like an accident. The scarf does the shaping, so the fold has to stay smooth from shoulder to hem.

The pairing rule is just as simple: keep everything else quiet and structured. High-waisted trousers or a midi skirt, a blazer over the top if the evening turns cool, and a shoe that grounds the softness — a pointed toe or a ballet flat. The moment the bottom gets loose and floaty too, the outfit loses its shape entirely.

Colour helps too. A printed silk scarf against plain black trousers reads quiet luxury; a solid chocolate brown scarf with cream wide-leg jeans reads minimalist and calm. One scarf, three folds — triangle halter, knotted bandeau, draped one-shoulder — and the same piece works for lunch, dinner, and a weekend out, with nothing else bought.
