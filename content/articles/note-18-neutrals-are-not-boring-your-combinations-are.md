Neutrals get blamed for boring outfits, but that's unfair. A head-to-toe camel look on the right person stops traffic. The problem is never beige itself — it's beige worn without intention: same tone top to bottom, same fabric, same safe silhouette.

Neutrals come alive through contrast. Mix warm camel with cool grey. Pair crisp white with soft cream — yes, they clash slightly, and that's the point. Add black for punctuation. Suddenly the "boring" palette has depth, temperature, rhythm.

Think of neutrals as an instrument, not a background. Played with one finger they're dull; played with the full range — light against dark, warm against cool, matte against shine — they're the most sophisticated sound in the room.
